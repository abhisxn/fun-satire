Phase B · Lane 2 · Subject Style Studies

One character. Ten movements.

TAI — the elderly Indian "everywoman" subject — reinterpreted across ten art movements and design traditions. Each card shows the same character in a different visual language, so we can compare how the registry should render her in HUD swatches and on canvas.

A · 01Swiss / Intl. Typographic

Clean Grid Authority

1950s · Müller-Brockmann · Akzidenz

Strict asymmetric grid, single Akzidenz-style sans serif, generous negative space, one signal accent color. Reads as institutional fact — neutral, objective, modern.

A · 02Brutalist

Concrete Bureaucracy

1970s · photocopied · monospace

Halftone screentone, off-register printing, stamped red marks, monospace type set like an internal memo. The aesthetic of a leaked government file — uncomfortable, true, undeniable.

A · 03Protest Poster · 1968

Enough

Pushpin · Bass · MacLean

Saturated saffron field, bold black silhouette, hand-cut display type, flat color planes. The visual language of the anti-war / civil rights movement — a poster that screams from a brick wall.

A · 04Risograph

Riso Duotone

indie print · fluorescent

Two-color run — fluorescent pink layered with teal at 0.6px misregistration, halftone dot field, paper grain. High-impact, low-fi, very on-trend. Cheap to render at any size; instantly recognizable.

Sharpie Wall

street · bleed · raw

Bold marker strokes with bleeding edges, scribbled hatching, high-saturation colors, splatter. Looks drawn on a napkin or a wall in thirty seconds. Honest, immediate, human.

A · 06Constructivist

Worker Hero

Rodchenko · Lissitzky · 1920s

Diagonal red-black composition, bold geometry, industrial display type, the silhouette of the common citizen. Treats TAI as the dignified subject of a revolutionary poster — serious, monumental.

Linocut

Wartime print · 2-color

Carved black ink lines, cross-hatched shading, two-color overlay (black + saffron), high contrast, hand-pulled feel. The look of a clandestine pamphlet — heavy, slow, deliberate.

Photocopied, distressed, cut-and-paste, hand-scrawled type, hot pink + black + neon. Pure DIY chaos — the visual language of a stapled zine passed hand to hand.
